How you should live your life

1

You should keep the number of people in your life to about 150.

You can know about 150 people well enough to remember what each has done and whether to trust them. Beyond that you are dealing with strangers, who don't know you and owe you nothing. People can leave your 150 and new ones can join, but you can't keep up with more.

For most of human history bands split and merged, but nobody had to keep up with thousands.

The science

2

You should live within walking distance of about fifty of them and see your closest five every day.

You feel safe when you see the same people every day. If you see your friends twice a month you will be lonely, even if they are good friends.

For most of human history you lived in a camp of a few dozen people and spent every day among them.

The science

3

You should rely on those people for food, childcare, labour, money, care when you are sick and protection, and do the same for them.

Friends who never need each other drift apart. If you pay strangers to mind your child and drive you to hospital, your friends never get asked, and nobody asks you either.

For most of human history there was nobody to pay. When you were sick or attacked, the people who fed and defended you were the ones you had done it for.

The scienceWhat really is: Friendship

4

You should keep your closest people for decades.

It takes years to learn how someone behaves when things go badly. If you move every few years, everyone you know is someone you met recently, and you trust none of them the way you trust an old friend.

For most of human history camps split up and re-formed again and again, but the people you trusted most stayed in your life for decades.

The science

5

You should get food, cook and eat with your people, and eat things you could have grown or caught yourselves.

People who eat alone most days feel low and often can't say why. Shopping, cooking and eating together puts you beside your people for hours every day, doing something all of you need done.

For most of human history people found food together and ate it at a shared fire.

6

You should choose a partner your people already know.

Your friends and family have watched this person for years and can tell you what they have seen. They also help the two of you when things get hard. With a partner none of them knows, you have to find out everything yourself and get through the hard times alone.

For most of human history couples formed inside a community where the families knew each other and helped feed the couple and their children.

7

You should have children where you have help and raise them among the adults and children of your 150. If you have no children, you should help raise theirs.

People stop wanting children when they can see no way to raise them: no help, no money, no safety. They are reading their situation correctly. Two parents alone with a baby are exhausted and anxious because a baby needs more adults than two.

For most of human history a baby was held and fed by its father, grandmothers, aunts and older children as well as its mother.

The scienceWhat really is: Parental burnout

8

You should do work that feeds, houses, protects, heals or raises people in your 150. If you can't, you should get as close as you can.

If your work goes to strangers and keeps nobody alive, you will feel it is pointless, even if it pays well. People who put food in front of someone they know don't have that feeling.

For most of human history there was no other kind of work. Everything you made or caught went to people you knew, and the group fed and protected the people it needed.

What really is: Purpose

9

You should spend most evenings sitting together, talking and making music.

This is when people settle arguments and hear what happened to each other that day. A group that never does it stops getting along.

For most of human history people spent the evening around a fire, talking and telling stories.

The science

10

You should spend most of the day outside and on foot, and sleep in the dark with no screen in the room.

Morning daylight decides when you get sleepy at night, and you sleep badly after a day of sitting. A phone by the bed shows you strangers and bad news while you are trying to fall asleep, and that keeps you alert.

For millions of years humans walked for hours a day to find food and slept in the dark beside people they knew.

The science: movementThe science: light

11

You should quit feeds, junk food, porn and gambling.

You scroll because you want company, and after an hour of scrolling you have had none. Porn does the same with sex, junk food with hunger and gambling with winning. You still want what you wanted before, so you go back.

For most of human history a sweet taste came from fruit or honey, and every face you saw belonged to a person in front of you.

12

You should compare yourself only with the people in your 150.

If you look at the richest and best-looking people out of millions every day, you will feel poor and ugly every day. Among 150 people you can be the one who is good at something they need.

For most of human history the only people you could compare yourself with were the ones around you.

The science

13

You should follow what happens to your 150. From the rest of the world, you should keep only news of a danger to your people that you can do something about.

You can help a person you know who is in trouble, and once you have helped you stop worrying. You can do nothing about a flood in a country you will never visit, so you keep worrying about it.

For most of human history news came from people you knew and was about people you knew.

The science

14

When you feel bad, you should find out what you are missing and start getting it the same day.

You feel bad because you are short of something: safety, food, sleep, company, a partner, children, or work that someone needs. The feeling stops when you get it, and most of those things come from other people. If you drink or scroll, you are still short of it and you feel worse the next day.

For most of human history what you needed was close by, and so were the people who could give it.
