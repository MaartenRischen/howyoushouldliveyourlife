The three checks, for every finding in Cor:

  1. Every finding is checked against the study's full text or abstract. Summaries, search snippets and Wikipedia don't count.
  2. The finding says no more than the study does.
  3. Every grade names the strongest study behind it and that study's main weakness.

A grade never goes up because it would help a page. A finding that failed to replicate is marked down, however widely it is cited. Cor keeps records of the evidence against itself, publishes the strongest attacks on its own work and names the results that would kill it.

How you should live your life

Start here

1

When you feel bad, you should find out what you are missing and start getting it the same day.

You feel bad because you are short of something: safety, food, sleep, company, touch, a partner, children, the respect of people who know you, or work that someone needs. The feeling stops when you get it, and most of those things come from other people. The other seventeen rules are the things people are most often short of.

For most of human history what you needed was close by, and so were the people who could give it.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou know which rules on this page you are furthest from, and you work on the biggest one every week.
  2. First stepThe next time you feel bad, you go down this page before you pick up your phone or a drink, and you ask which rule you are furthest from.

Why you feel so bad

Your people

2

You should keep the number of people in your life to about 150.

You can know about 150 people well enough to remember what each has done and whether to trust them. Beyond that you are dealing with strangers, who don't know you and owe you nothing. People can leave your 150 and new ones can join, but you can't keep up with more.

For most of human history bands split and merged, but nobody had to keep up with thousands.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou can name the people in your life, and they get most of your time. Followers, group chats and contacts you will never meet get what is left.
  2. First stepYou write down everyone you would help if they asked and who would help you. That list is your people, however short it is.

The science

3

You should live within walking distance of about fifty of them and see your closest five every day.

You feel safe when you see the same people every day. If you see your friends twice a month you will be lonely, even if they are good friends. A day among strangers on the train, in the street and at work keeps you on guard.

For most of human history you lived in a camp of a few dozen people and spent every day among them. A stranger was rare.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYour closest five live within walking distance and you see them most days.
  2. Before thatOne or two of your closest people live on your street or in your building.
  3. First stepYou eat with the same few friends on a fixed evening every week.

If you have no people yet: go where the same people turn up every week, such as a team, a choir, a workshop or a neighbourhood group, and keep going back.

The scienceWhat really is: Shyness

4

You should rely on those people for food, childcare, labour, money, care when you are sick and protection, and do the same for them.

Friends who never need each other drift apart. If you pay strangers to mind your child and drive you to hospital, your friends never get asked, and nobody asks you either.

For most of human history there was nobody to pay. When you were sick or attacked, the people who fed and defended you were the ones you had done it for.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestA few households near you share meals and childcare most days and lend each other money, tools and cars.
  2. Before thatOne other household has your key, minds your children every week and is the first you call when you are sick.
  3. First stepThe next time you would pay a stranger to babysit, move your things or drive you to the airport, you ask someone you know. When they ask you, you say yes.

The scienceWhat really is: Friendship

5

You should keep your closest people for decades.

It takes years to learn how someone behaves when things go badly. If you move every few years, everyone you know is someone you met recently, and you trust none of them the way you trust an old friend. People did change camps. If the people around you are bad for you, you should leave, find better ones and stay with those.

For most of human history camps split up and re-formed again and again, but the people you trusted most stayed in your life for decades.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou stay in one place, or move together with your people, so the friends you have now are still near you in twenty years.
  2. Before thatBefore you move for a job, you count what it costs. The people you would leave took years to know.
  3. First stepYou get back in touch with the people who have known you longest, and you see them in person this year.

The science

Your days

6

You should get food, cook and eat with your people, and eat things you could have grown or caught yourselves.

People who eat alone most days feel low and often can't say why. Shopping, cooking and eating together puts you beside your people for hours every day, doing something all of you need done.

For most of human history food moved between households. You ate what others in the camp had caught, carried or cooked, and they ate yours.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou eat your main meal with your people most days, and your households take turns cooking for each other.
  2. Before thatYou cook for another household once a week and they cook for you.
  3. First stepYou eat one meal a day with someone you know.

The science

7

You should spend most evenings sitting together, talking, making music and playing games.

This is when people settle arguments and hear what happened to each other that day. A group that never does it stops getting along.

For most of human history people spent the evening around a fire, talking and telling stories.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestA few evenings a week you sit with the same people, with no screen on, until someone goes to bed.
  2. First stepOne evening this week you turn off the screens and ask someone over. You talk, play a game or play music.

The scienceWhat really is: Gossip

8

You should touch your people every day and sleep beside someone. If you have a baby, you should carry it and sleep beside it.

Touch from someone you trust calms you more than their words do, and no call or message carries it. A baby that sleeps alone in a crib goes without that contact for the whole night.

For most of human history babies were carried on the body by day, and adults sat, worked and slept close to each other.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou hug, lean against or sit close to the people you live with every day, and you share a bed or a room.
  2. First stepYou touch one person you trust today: a hug when you arrive, an arm around a shoulder, a hand on a back.

Sleep beside your baby on a firm, flat surface. Never on a sofa or armchair, and never after alcohol or drugs.

The science: touchThe science: sleep

9

You should spend most of the day outside and on foot, and sleep in the dark with no screen in the room.

Morning daylight decides when you get sleepy at night, and you sleep badly after a day of sitting. A phone by the bed shows you strangers and bad news while you are trying to fall asleep, and that keeps you alert.

For millions of years humans walked for hours a day to find food and slept in the dark beside people they knew.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou walk to most of the places you go, you are outside for a few hours every day, and your phone spends the night in another room.
  2. First stepYou go outside within an hour of waking, and tonight the phone charges outside the bedroom.

Your place among them

10

You should be someone your people come to for something, and you should compare yourself only with them.

You need the respect of people who know you. You get it by being good at something they need: fixing things, cooking, calming a fight, making people laugh. You find out whether you are good at it by comparing yourself with the people around you, and that kind of comparing is healthy. Respect from people you see face to face goes with feeling well more than income or education does. If you compare yourself every day with the richest and best-looking people out of millions, you will feel poor and ugly every day.

For most of human history your standing was set by the few dozen people you lived with, who had watched what you did for years.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestA few people you see every week rely on you for one thing, and they tell you so.
  2. Before thatYou pick one thing you are good at and do it for people you know, for free, until they start asking.
  3. First stepYou stop following people you will never meet whose lives make yours look small.

11

You should do work that feeds, houses, protects, heals or raises people in your 150.

If your work goes to strangers and you never see what it did for anyone, it is hard to care about, even if it pays well. People who put food in front of someone they know don't have that problem. You also need to see the result of your own effort, and to do more than one kind of thing in a day. The worst work earns its money by hooking people on feeds, gambling or junk food.

For most of human history there was no other kind of work. Everything you made or caught went to people you knew, and the group fed and protected the people it needed.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou make or repair what that work needs, teach the skills, or hold your people together by settling disputes, telling the stories and making the music.
  2. Before thatYour paid work is for strangers, and outside it you do one of those things for people you know every week.
  3. First stepThis week you do one piece of work for someone you know: cook for them, fix something, mind their child.

12

You should take orders only from people you can refuse or walk away from.

A boss you can't answer back to and can't afford to leave keeps you afraid and angry every working day. You can still follow someone who is good at the task, because you chose to, it covers that task only, and you can stop.

For most of human history bands had no rulers. People followed whoever was best at the task for as long as they chose to, mocked or shunned anyone who tried to dominate, and could leave for another camp.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou work for yourself or with people you chose, and the group talks through the decisions that affect it.
  2. Before thatYou have enough saved, and enough people who would take you in, that you could quit tomorrow.
  3. First stepYou work out what it would take for you to be able to walk away from your job, in money and in people.

The science

13

When something threatens you, you should act until it is over.

Fear is built to make you do something, and it stops when the danger is gone. A debt, a lawsuit or a job you dread never ends, so the fear never stops, and years of it make you ill. You should end a threat if you can, and get away from it if you can't.

For most of human history a danger was a predator, a fight or a fall. It was over in minutes, and afterwards you were back among your people.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestNothing has been hanging over you for more than a few weeks. When something comes up, you deal with it or you leave.
  2. Before thatYou take the threat that has been with you longest and end it, even at a cost: pay off the debt, settle the dispute, quit the job.
  3. First stepYou write down what you are afraid of and, next to each one, the next thing you can do about it. You do one of them today.

The science

Family

14

You should find a partner among people who know you both, and you should let your people have a say.

Couples whose family and friends approve of the relationship last longer. Your people have watched this person for years and can tell you what they have seen, and they help the two of you when things get hard. On a dating app you choose among strangers from a photo, and on one large dating site most people got a handful of messages while a few got hundreds.

For most of human history a partner came from a few hundred people who were known before anything started, each with a known family and a reputation. In most forager societies on record, parents or close kin arranged the marriage. Among foragers many couples split, and the children still had their people around them.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou meet partners through friends, family and the places you go every week, and you bring someone new to your people early.
  2. First stepYou tell your friends and family you are looking, and you ask them who they know.

If you already have a partner: bring them into your people, so that they eat with you, help and get helped.

15

You should have children where you have help and raise them among the adults and children of your 150. If you have no children, you should help raise theirs. You should keep your old people among you.

People stop wanting children when they can see no way to raise them: no help, no money, no safety. They are reading their situation correctly. Two parents alone with a baby are exhausted and anxious because a baby needs more adults than two. A child needs the same few carers for years, and daycare staff who change can't be that. Children learn to deal with people by playing with other children of all ages, without adults running it. Old people who live apart are missing from all of this, and so is what they would give: food, care and what they know.

For most of human history a baby was held and fed by its father, grandmothers, aunts and older children as well as its mother. In five forager groups women had their first child at about 19 or 20, and those who lived through their fertile years had about six births. In the United States in 2023 the average age at a first birth was 27.5.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou live near grandparents or close friends who mind your children several days a week, and your children play outside with other children of all ages.
  2. Before thatA few households take turns with each other's children every week, so every child has the same few adults.
  3. First stepYou ask one person you trust to mind your child for an afternoon this week. If you have no children, you offer that afternoon to a parent you know.

The world outside your people

16

You should follow what happens to your 150. From the rest of the world, you should keep only news of a danger to your people that you can do something about.

You can help a person you know who is in trouble, and once you have helped you stop worrying. You can do nothing about a flood in a country you will never visit, so you keep worrying about it.

For most of human history news came from people you knew and was about people you knew.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou hear your news from people you know, and you check the wider news once a week for anything you need to act on.
  2. First stepYou turn off the news alerts on your phone, and you ask one person you know how they are doing.

The science

17

You should take sides only among people you know.

Your mind sorts people into us and them within minutes, on any label it is given. A party, a nation or a football club gives you a side of millions who share no life with you, and an enemy of millions you will never meet. You get the anger of a fight and nothing you can do to end it. In the United States the gap between how warmly people feel about their own party and the other one grew from 23 to 41 points out of 100 between 1978 and 2016.

For most of human history the largest group a person counted as us held a few hundred to a few thousand people, who shared a language, married each other and could be reached on foot.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestThe groups you belong to are ones you turn up for in person: a team you play in, a street, a congregation, a union branch.
  2. First stepYou stop arguing online about parties and clubs, and you put that hour into something you and people you know can change.

Leave behind

18

You should quit feeds, porn, junk food, gambling, alcohol, drugs, following famous strangers, and psychiatric medication where no physical cause was found.

Each of these gives you a feeling that was built for a real thing, without the thing, and stronger than the real thing ever gave it. Feeds stand in for company, porn for sex with a partner, junk food for food, gambling for winning something, and famous strangers and series characters for friends. Alcohol gives you the feeling of being safe, heroin and painkillers the feeling of being close to someone, and cocaine and speed the feeling of going after something that matters. A pill for a low or anxious feeling changes the feeling and leaves in place whatever the feeling was about. All of them are hard to stop while the real thing is missing, and easy to leave once it is back.

For most of human history a sweet taste came from fruit or honey, every face you saw belonged to a person in front of you, and the feeling of being safe or close came from being safe or close.

How to get thereHide the steps
  1. Next bestYou cut each one down as the real thing comes in, at the speed it comes in.
  2. Before thatYou start on the real thing first, through the first steps of the rules above: people near you, meals together, touch, work someone needs.
  3. First stepFor each one you use, you write down what it stands in for.

Stopping alcohol or some medications at once is dangerous. Bring those down step by step with a doctor.
